We shall start by assuming that the friction value being defined is (eventually) the friction argument for the physx vehicle 2 tire model. But, that is a USD detail.

In order to simplify calculations, let define crop and road friction values over the entire farm, but only sample the relevant field over the meshed sub-regions for crop and road. This should be clean, because we are sampling the triangles at their centroids - none of which land on crop or road boundaries. Out first task is to generate a field that follows a power law:

Define the spatial friction variation as a 2D random field with an isotropic power spectrum.

User-facing parameters (one for crop, one for road):

friction_mean
friction_variation_range
friction_scale_m
friction_spectral_slope
friction_seed

Let:

L0 = friction_scale_m

If spatial frequency is represented in cycles per meter, define:

f0 = 1 / L0

For a 2D spatial frequency with components fx and fy:

f = sqrt(fx² + fy²)

Use the target power spectrum:

P(f) ∝ [1 + (f / f0)²]^(-β / 2)

where:

β = friction_spectral_slope

Interpretation:

L0 is the characteristic spatial scale where the spectrum transitions from approximately flat at large spatial scales to approximately power-law behavior at smaller spatial scales.
For f << f0, P(f) is approximately constant.
For f >> f0, P(f) is approximately proportional to f^(-β).
Larger β produces a smoother field with less high-spatial-frequency variation.
Larger L0 produces larger spatial patches of similar friction.

A practical FFT implementation can:

Generate a 2D white-noise field.
FFT the field.
Compute f = sqrt(fx² + fy²) for each Fourier component.
Multiply each Fourier coefficient by sqrt(P(f)).
Inverse FFT to obtain the spatial field.
Normalize or smoothly bound the resulting field.
Map it to the requested friction range around friction_mean.

To prevent aliasing, we should also truncate the field to the nyquist, which is rougly twice the value in farm_resolution_m (see above).

The requested friction interval is:

friction_min = friction_mean - friction_variation_range / 2

friction_max = friction_mean + friction_variation_range / 2

The continuous friction field should be deterministic for a given friction_seed.

For implementation with numpy.fft.fftfreq(), use frequencies in cycles per meter directly. This avoids introducing 2π factors internally.
